\begin{abstract}
We formalize the classical sphere-covering lower bound on the covering number $K_q(n,R)$ in Lean~4, against Mathlib~v4.34.1.
The library proves that every code over $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ of length $n$ which covers the space at Hamming radius $R$ has at least $\lceil q^n/V\rceil$ words, where $V$ is the size of a Hamming ball, and it checks eight numerical instances, including $K_7(9,4)\ge 221$.
The same archive determines several small covering numbers exactly, among them $K_2(7,1)=16$ by the binary Hamming code of length~$7$, and it exhibits a $12$-word covering of $(\mathbb{F}_2)^6$ at radius~$1$.
It does not prove matching upper bounds for the eight instances, does not decide whether those lower bounds improve the literature, and leaves $K_2(6,1)\ge 11$ conditional on a search the kernel did not finish.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The covering number $K_q(n,R)$ is the least cardinality of a code $C\subseteq A^n$, with $|A|=q$, such that every word of $A^n$ lies at Hamming distance at most $R$ from some word of $C$.
The sphere-covering bound, already in Hamming and in the form recorded by Cohen, Honkala, Litsyn and Lobstein, says that if $V$ is the number of words in a ball of radius $R$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sphere}
  q^n \le K_q(n,R)\cdot V,
\end{equation}
and therefore $K_q(n,R)\ge \lceil q^n/V\rceil$.
The inequality is classical.
What is not automatic is the arithmetic: for the cells in Table~\ref{tab:cells}, $V$ has up to six digits and $q^n$ up to eight, and a hand evaluation of the ceiling is a place to slip.

This note reports a machine-checked proof of the bound and of eight of those ceilings, in the Lean~4 theorem prover~\cite{lean4} on top of Mathlib~\cite{mathlib}.
The source that was built is the tag \texttt{v0.\allowbreak 2.\allowbreak 0} of \url{https://github.com/thiagopatzdorf/Matematica}.
A theorem below is a claim only when it is the name of a declaration in that tree and that declaration is imported by \texttt{CoveringLean.\allowbreak lean}.
Two files in the tree, \texttt{A5b\_\allowbreak Stress.\allowbreak lean} and \texttt{A6d\_\allowbreak SearchHeavy.\allowbreak lean}, are not imported.
Nothing in them is claimed.

\section{Definitions}
In the development \texttt{CoveringA2} the alphabet is $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ and a word is a function $\mathtt{Fin}\, n \to \mathtt{ZMod}\, q$.
Distance is Mathlib's \texttt{hammingDist}.
The ball of radius $R$ about $c$ is the finite set of words at distance at most $R$ from $c$, and $C$ covers at radius $R$ when every word of the space lies in the ball of some element of $C$.
The closed volume used for the numerical bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
  V(q,n,R) = \sum_{i=0}^{R} \binom{n}{i}(q-1)^i,
\end{equation}
which is \texttt{CoveringA3.\allowbreak V}.
The eight final statements do not define $K_q(n,R)$.
Each says: every covering code has at least the stated number of words.
That is the lower bound $K_q(n,R)\ge c$, not the determination of $K_q(n,R)$.

A second development, \texttt{CoveringA6}, uses the alphabet $\mathtt{Fin}\, q$ rather than $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$, still with Mathlib's Hamming distance.
There \texttt{IsK}\,$q$\,$n$\,$R$\,$k$ is the proposition that some code of size $k$ covers and no code of size less than $k$ does, so it is the equality $K_q(n,R)=k$.
The two developments are not fused into one definition of $K$.
The statements below name the development they come from.

\section{The bound}
The argument in the library has four steps, each a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[volume]
The number of words of Hamming weight at most $R$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^n$ equals $V(q,n,R)$.
The generating function $\sum_x X^{\mathrm{wt}(x)} = \bigl(1+(q-1)X\bigr)^n$ is \texttt{CoveringA1.\allowbreak weight\_\allowbreak genfun}; the ball at the origin is \texttt{CoveringA1.\allowbreak ball\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak card}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[translation]
For every centre $c$, the ball of radius $R$ about $c$ has the same cardinality as the ball about the origin (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak ball\_\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak indep}).
The space itself has cardinality $q^n$ (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak space}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[sphere covering]\label{thm:sphere}
If $C$ covers at radius $R$, then $q^n \le |C|\cdot |B(0,R)|$ (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak covering}).
If that volume does not divide $q^n$, the inequality is strict (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak perfect}).
In the natural numbers, $\lceil q^n/V\rceil \le |C|$ (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak ceil\_\allowbreak bound}).
Equality $|C|\cdot V = q^n$ forces the balls about the codewords to be pairwise disjoint (\texttt{CoveringA2.\allowbreak equality\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak perfect}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[identification]
\texttt{CoveringChain.\allowbreak ball\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak V} identifies the geometric ball of \texttt{CoveringA2}, cut out by \texttt{hammingDist}, with the closed sum~\eqref{eq:V}.
\texttt{CoveringChain.\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak covering\_\allowbreak formula} is Theorem~\ref{thm:sphere} with that sum in place of the abstract volume.
\end{theorem}

The eight numerical theorems apply the formula to a cell whose volume and ceiling have been evaluated.
For each row of Table~\ref{tab:cells}, \texttt{CoveringA3.\allowbreak cell\_\allowbreak $\ldots$} packages the equalities $V(q,n,R)=v$ and $\lceil q^n/v\rceil = c$, together with the one-step obstruction $q^n \not\le (c-1)\,v$.
The obstruction says that the ceiling is sharp as a consequence of the sphere bound.
It does not say that a code of size $c$ exists.

In particular, every covering of $(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^9$ at radius $4$ has at least $221$ words.
The library contains no theorem $K_7(9,4)\le 1351$, and it contains no list of $1351$ words.

\section{Exact values}
On top of the lower bound, the library determines a few covering numbers completely.

\begin{theorem}
If $R \ge n$, then one word covers and $K_q(n,R)=1$ (\texttt{CoveringA4.\allowbreak K\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak one\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le}).
For $n\ge 1$, $K_q(n,n-1)=q$: there is a covering of size $q$, and every covering has at least $q$ words (\texttt{CoveringA4.\allowbreak K\_\allowbreak pred\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak q}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}
In \texttt{CoveringA6}, the following equalities hold:
$K_2(2,1)=2$, $K_2(3,1)=2$, $K_2(4,1)=4$, $K_3(3,2)=3$, and $K_2(7,1)=16$.
\end{theorem}

The last of these is the binary Hamming code of length $7$.
It is defined as the kernel of the parity-check matrix whose columns are $1,\ldots,7$.
The kernel checks that the code has $16$ words, that it covers at radius $1$, that every ball of radius $1$ has $8$ words, that $|C|\cdot 8 = 2^7$, and that distinct codewords have disjoint balls.
The sphere bound is therefore met with equality, and no code of size $15$ covers.

\begin{theorem}
The twelve words of $(\mathbb{F}_2)^6$ with integer encodings
\[
  0,1,2,15,28,23,57,58,59,39,44,52
\]
form a code of cardinality $12$ which covers at radius $1$ (\texttt{CoveringA6.\allowbreak code12\_\allowbreak card}, \texttt{CoveringA6.\allowbreak code12\_\allowbreak covers}).
Hence $K_2(6,1)\le 12$.
\end{theorem}

\section{What the kernel accepted}
The build that this note refers to is \texttt{lake build} of \texttt{CoveringLean} at Lean~4.34.1 and Mathlib tag \texttt{v4.\allowbreak 34.\allowbreak 1}.
On 1 October 2026, on a virtual machine of type \texttt{e2-standard-8}, that build finished $8934$ jobs in $34$ seconds, with a peak resident set of $7.6$\,GB.
The library imports no \texttt{sorry} and no \texttt{native\_\allowbreak decide}.
Declarations proved by \texttt{decide +kernel} were reduced in the kernel, not by a native evaluator.
\texttt{\#print axioms} on \texttt{CoveringChain.\allowbreak sphere\_\allowbreak covering\_\allowbreak formula} and on each of the eight \texttt{SPH\_\allowbreak K*\_\allowbreak lb} theorems reports at most \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.\allowbreak choice} and \texttt{Quot.\allowbreak sound}.

Rebuilding requires \texttt{elan}, then \texttt{lake exe cache get} and \texttt{lake build} in the source tree.
Running \texttt{decide +kernel} by hand on the universe of a large function type is a different computation: it asked for $17$ to $24$\,GB and is not part of the build above.

\section{What is not a theorem}
The archive does not prove an upper bound for any row of Table~\ref{tab:cells}.
A lower bound of $221$ is not a construction, and it is not a claim that $221$ is the covering number.

The statement ``no code of size at most $10$ covers $(\mathbb{F}_2)^6$ at radius $1$'' is \texttt{CoveringA6.\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak cover\_\allowbreak 2\_\allowbreak 6\_\allowbreak 1\_\allowbreak le10}, and its hypothesis is
\[
  \texttt{refuted}\; 64\; 7\; \texttt{bm6}\; \texttt{nbr6}\; 10\; 0 \;=\; \texttt{true}.
\]
The file that would discharge that hypothesis by a kernel decision, \texttt{A6d\_\allowbreak SearchHeavy.\allowbreak lean}, is not imported, does not build, and ran out of memory when it was tried.
Until that equality is itself a theorem, $K_2(6,1)\ge 11$ is not a theorem.
The unconditional facts at this parameter are the sphere-covering method in general and the explicit upper bound $K_2(6,1)\le 12$.

The library does not compare Table~\ref{tab:cells} with the published tables of covering numbers, and it does not prove that any of the eight ceilings is new.
The sphere bound is not new.
The checked object is the proof.
